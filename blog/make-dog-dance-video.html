Tips for Getting the Best Dog Photos

The photo you choose has a big impact on the final video quality. A great photo means the AI has accurate information about your dog's appearance and can produce a more convincing result. Here is what to look for:

Tip: Go for Natural Lighting

Outdoor photos in daylight are ideal. If you are shooting indoors, position your dog near a window. Soft, even light lets the AI clearly read your dog's features — fur color, facial markings, and eye placement all come through better without harsh shadows or yellow indoor light casting a tint.

Tip: Get the Face in Frame

The AI needs a clear view of your dog's face to preserve their identity in the video. A photo where your dog is looking at the camera (or close to it) gives the best result. Avoid photos where their face is turned away, buried in a blanket, or partially cut off by the edge of the frame.

Tip: Show Some Body

Pure face closeups can work, but photos that show at least the head, neck, and shoulders give the AI more body geometry to work with. Full-body photos tend to produce the most natural-looking movement because the AI has the complete shape of your dog to reference when generating the dance.

Tip: Skip the Blurry Action Shots

That mid-run photo of your dog is adorable, but motion blur makes it harder for the AI to reconstruct a clean representation of your dog's appearance. Use a still, in-focus photo. Most camera apps have a portrait mode that works well for this.

Tip: Avoid Costumes and Accessories

Photos where your dog is wearing a Halloween costume, glasses, or a complicated harness can confuse the AI about your dog's actual body shape. A plain photo of your dog as they normally look will produce better results than one with a lot of added props.

Which Dance Styles Work Best for Dogs

All 16 dance styles are available for dog videos, and they all produce entertaining results — but some styles tend to get particularly strong reactions. Here are a few worth trying first:

  • Hip-Hop — The loose, groove-based movements translate really well to dog body mechanics. The result tends to look both convincing and hilarious in equal measure.
  • Salsa — Hip-swinging Latin footwork on a dog is a reliable crowd-pleaser. The rhythm-forward choreography gives the AI a lot to work with and the videos tend to be highly shareable.
  • TikTok Viral — Built for social media sharing, and dog videos performing trending moves perform extremely well on TikTok and Instagram Reels.
  • K-Pop — The sharp, synchronized choreography looks unexpectedly good on dogs. The contrast between the precise moves and the dog subject is part of what makes it funny.
  • Samba — High energy and rhythmically dense. Dog samba videos have a way of stopping people mid-scroll.

If you are not sure where to start, Hip-Hop and TikTok Viral are the two styles that consistently produce the most shareable dog dance content. That said, the best way to find your favorite is to try a few — each style generates a completely different video from the same photo.
