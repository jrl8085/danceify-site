A TikTok dance video normally needs someone to learn the dance and film it. Danceify skips both: you give it one photo and it makes a short clip of that person (or pet) dancing. This guide covers the whole path from photo to posted video, which photos work, where the music comes from, and what it costs. It does not cover filming yourself; for that, TikTok's own camera is the tool.

How do I make the video, step by step?

01 Download Danceify from the App Store

Get Danceify - AI Dance Videos (App Store ID 6760788471). It is free to download. There is no account, sign-up or email; purchases go through your Apple Account.

02 Tap the tab for who is in the photo

On Home, choose DOG, CAT, BABY or ADULT. The app uses this to apply the right body proportions and movement.

03 Pick the TikTok style

Choose TikTok Viral from the 17 dance styles. It is the style built around the arm patterns, body rolls and direct-to-camera energy that short vertical clips use. The next section lists other styles worth trying.

04 Choose one photo and a track

Pick a photo from your camera roll. One person or pet, whole body visible, good light, facing the camera. Then pick a track in the MUSIC row.

05 Tap CREATE

Danceify makes a 10-second vertical dance video. A Regular video uses 250 coins in app version 5.38 and later (150 coins in earlier versions). You can change the track on the player once the video is ready.

06 Save it and post it to TikTok

Save the video to your camera roll. Open TikTok, tap +, choose Upload, pick the video, add a sound, caption or hashtags if you want, and post. Because the video is already vertical and about 10 seconds long, nothing needs cropping or trimming.

Which dance styles suit TikTok?

Every Danceify video is vertical and 10 seconds long, so all 17 styles can be posted. These four tend to read best on a phone screen:

  • TikTok Viral: upper-body-heavy, camera-facing choreography made for this format. Start here.
  • K-Pop: sharp, synchronized moves that look clean at small sizes.
  • Hip-hop: loose grooves and isolations; works well on dogs and babies too.
  • Reggaeton: hip-led rhythm that fits short, looping clips.

The other styles (EDM Shuffle, Salsa, Bollywood, Samba, Greek, Ballet, Tango, Flamenco, Swing, Belly Dance, Hula, Country Line, Irish) are just as valid; a ballet-dancing cat is its own kind of TikTok post. See all 17 styles.

What photo should I use?

The photo decides most of the result. Three rules cover almost everything:

One person or pet

The app expects a single subject. Crop a group photo down to one person before you pick it.

Whole body visible

Head to feet in the frame, with a little space around. Whole-body photos dance best; the app warns you when only part of a person's body is showing. For a dog or cat, a photo showing the whole animal, sitting or standing, is enough: the app stands them up for the dance.

Good light, facing the camera

Daylight or a bright room, no backlighting, no sunglasses, no heavy filters. A front-facing or slightly angled shot gives the app a clear face to keep.

Avoid screenshots of screenshots and very small images; use the original photo from your camera roll. For more on photos, see photo to dance video.

Where does the music come from?

Danceify includes tracks. You pick one in the MUSIC row before tapping CREATE, and you can change it on the player after the video is made. The video you save to your camera roll has that track in it.

TikTok also lets you add one of its own sounds when you upload, the same way as with any video from your camera roll. If you want a trending TikTok sound, add it in TikTok at upload time.

Can I make a dog, cat or baby TikTok dance?

Yes. The DOG, CAT and BABY tabs on Home exist for exactly this. Use one photo of the pet or baby with the whole body visible, choose the TikTok style, and tap CREATE. The app keeps the pet's markings and the baby's face. For photo tips specific to pets, see dog dance video tips and AI cat dance; for babies, baby dance video tips. Only upload a photo of another person if they have agreed, and a photo of a child only if you are the child's parent or legal guardian.
