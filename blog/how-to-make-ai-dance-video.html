Tips for the Best Results

Pro Tip: Lighting

Natural daylight produces the best results. If shooting indoors, face a window or use even, diffused lighting. Avoid harsh shadows across the face — the AI needs to clearly map facial features to preserve identity in the video.

Pro Tip: Photo Angle

Front-facing photos work best. Slight angles (up to about 30 degrees) are fine, but extreme side profiles or overhead shots can confuse the AI. The closer to a straight-on portrait, the better the dance output will look.

Pro Tip: Subject Framing

The subject should fill a good portion of the frame. Headshots work, but waist-up or full-body shots give the AI more to work with and tend to produce more natural-looking dance movements. Leave a bit of space around the subject rather than cropping too tight.

Pro Tip: Pet Photos

For dogs and cats, use a photo where the pet is looking toward the camera. Clear shots of their face and body give the AI enough detail to preserve breed-specific features like fur markings, ear shape, and coloring. The AI will render them standing on their hind legs with choreography designed for their body type.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Group photos — The AI expects one subject per image. Crop to a single person or pet.
  • Sunglasses or masks — Anything that covers the face makes identity preservation harder.
  • Very low resolution — Avoid screenshots of screenshots or heavily compressed images. Use the original photo from your camera roll.
  • Dark or backlit photos — If you can barely see the subject's face, the AI will struggle too.
  • Heavy filters — Beauty filters and heavy edits can distort facial geometry. Use the original, unfiltered photo.
