Step-by-Step: Make an AI Dance Video From a Photo

01 Download Danceify From the App Store

Go to the Apple App Store and download Danceify. It is a free download.

02 Open the App and Get Your Coins

Launch Danceify. A brief onboarding screen explains how the app works. You will not be asked to create an account or verify an email address. At the end, start Creation Weekly with its introductory offer; you confirm it with your Apple Account, and your coins are ready to use. If you would rather not subscribe, buy a coin pack instead.

03 Choose Your Subject Type

Danceify asks what kind of subject is in your photo: adult, baby, dog, or cat. Select the correct type. The AI uses this to apply the right body mechanics and proportions — a person and a dog require fundamentally different movement physics, so this selection matters for output quality.

04 Upload Your Photo

Select a photo from your camera roll. For the best result, use a clear, well-lit image where the subject is front-facing and takes up a good portion of the frame. One subject per photo. Avoid heavy filters, extreme angles, or images with busy backgrounds. The clearer and simpler the photo, the better the AI output.

05 Pick a Dance Style

Choose from 17 styles including EDM Shuffle, Salsa, Hip-Hop, K-Pop, TikTok Viral, Ballet, Bollywood, Samba, Tango, Flamenco, Reggaeton, Swing, Belly Dance, Hula, Greek Dance, Country Line, and Irish Dance. Each style produces a completely different video from the same photo, so if you enjoy the first one, there are more to explore.

06 Generate Your Video

Tap the generate button. The result is a short, vertical video of your subject performing the dance you selected.

07 Save and Share

Preview your video, add a music track from the in-app selection, and save it to your camera roll. It is ready to post directly to TikTok, Instagram Reels, or wherever you want to share it.

Photo Tips That Make a Difference

Since every video uses coins, it is worth taking a moment to pick the right photo before you generate. The single biggest factor in video quality is the source image.

  • Lighting: Daylight or well-lit indoor photos produce significantly better results than dark or backlit images. The AI needs to clearly see the subject's face and body.
  • Angle: Front-facing or close to front-facing works best. Profiles and extreme overhead angles reduce output quality.
  • Framing: Waist-up or full-body shots give the AI more to work with than tight headshots. Leave a little space around the subject.
  • Filters: Skip the heavy filters. Use the original, unedited photo from your camera roll for the cleanest AI output.
  • Focus: Make sure the subject is in focus. Blurry photos produce blurry-looking dance videos.
