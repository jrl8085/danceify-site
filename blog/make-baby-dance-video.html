Photo Tips for Baby Dance Videos

Babies are not always cooperative subjects for photography, but even a decent candid shot can produce a great dance video. Here is what to aim for and what to avoid:

Tip: Bright, Even Lighting

Natural light near a window is ideal. Avoid dim rooms, harsh overhead lighting, or flash photos with strong shadows. The AI needs a clear view of your baby's facial features — good lighting is the single biggest factor in getting a recognizable result.

Tip: Face Toward Camera

A photo where your baby is looking toward the camera (or close to it) works much better than one where they are looking away. You do not need perfect eye contact — even a slight angle is fine — but a clear view of their face lets the AI preserve their identity accurately.

Tip: Show the Upper Body

Pure face closeups can work, but photos that show the head, neck, shoulders, and ideally the torso give the AI more body geometry to work with. The dance animation is applied across the whole body, so more body in the frame means more convincing movement in the output.

Tip: Simple Backgrounds Work Best

A photo with a clean or simple background helps the AI focus on the subject. Busy backgrounds (lots of clutter, other people moving) are not disqualifying, but they can occasionally distract the processing. A baby on a white blanket or in front of a plain wall will consistently produce clean results.

Tip: Use Recent, In-Focus Photos

Babies change quickly. A photo that accurately represents what your baby looks like right now will produce the most recognizable video. Avoid old, low-resolution photos or screenshots. Use the original from your camera roll, not a filtered version or a screenshot of a screenshot.

Tip: One Baby Per Photo

The AI works with one subject per image. If the photo has twins, or your baby is with an older sibling, crop the image so only the baby you want to animate is in the frame. Multiple subjects in the same photo can confuse the subject detection.

Best Dance Styles for Baby Videos

All 16 dance styles work with the baby subject mode, and they all produce entertaining results. That said, some styles tend to get particularly strong reactions for baby content:

  • Hip-Hop — The groove-forward choreography is loose and expressive, which translates naturally to baby proportions.
  • TikTok Viral — Built specifically for social media virality. Baby dance videos performing trending moves are a proven format on TikTok and Reels.
  • Bollywood — The expressive, gesture-rich choreography looks unexpectedly wonderful on babies. The colorful energy of Bollywood dance adapts really well to the baby body type.
  • Salsa — The rhythmic, hip-forward movement in Salsa produces memorable results. Baby Salsa is the kind of video that gets forwarded to people who were not even expecting a video.
  • K-Pop — Sharp, synchronized choreography. The contrast between precise K-Pop moves and baby proportions creates a result that is both technically impressive and deeply funny.

If you are sharing to a broader family audience — grandparents, aunts and uncles, extended family group chats — Bollywood and Salsa tend to have the widest appeal. If you are posting to social media for a younger audience, TikTok Viral and Hip-Hop are the better starting points.

Ideas for Sharing Baby Dance Videos

Baby dance videos perform extremely well on social media and in personal sharing contexts alike. A few ways people use them:

  • Family group chats: The most common use. Save to camera roll, send via iMessage or WhatsApp. Grandparents reliably react well to these.
  • Birth announcements and milestone celebrations: A dance video of a newborn or a baby hitting a milestone (first birthday, first steps) makes for a memorable shareable moment.
  • Social media accounts: If you run an account for your baby or family, AI dance videos are a consistent source of high-engagement content with minimal effort.
  • Personalized gifts: Create a dance video using a baby photo and share it as part of a birthday or holiday message to family members who might not otherwise know about the app.
