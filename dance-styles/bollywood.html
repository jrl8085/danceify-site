About Bollywood Dance

Bollywood dance is the vibrant, high-energy style that accompanies the song-and-dance sequences in Indian Hindi-language cinema. The term "Bollywood" itself is a portmanteau of Bombay (now Mumbai) and Hollywood, and the dance tradition it describes is equally hybrid — a kaleidoscopic fusion of classical Indian dance forms, folk traditions from across the subcontinent, and Western influences ranging from jazz to hip-hop to Latin movement.

The roots of Bollywood choreography lie in India's classical dance traditions: Bharatanatyam from Tamil Nadu, with its geometric precision and elaborate hand gestures (mudras); Kathak from North India, known for its intricate footwork and spinning turns (chakkars); and Odissi from Odisha, celebrated for its fluid tribhanga (three-bend) posture. Early Bollywood films drew heavily from these forms, but by the 1970s and 1980s, choreographers began incorporating disco, Western pop dance, and folk styles like bhangra (the energetic harvest dance of Punjab) and garba (the circular stick dance of Gujarat).

What makes Bollywood dance unique is its theatrical expressiveness. Every movement tells a story. Abhinaya — the art of expression — is paramount. Eyebrows lift, hands gesture meaningfully, the entire body becomes a vehicle for emotion. A Bollywood routine might transition from coy flirtation to triumphant celebration to dramatic heartbreak within a single song, each shift conveyed through movement as much as through lyrics.
