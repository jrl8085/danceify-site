About Belly Dance

Belly dance — known in Arabic as raqs sharqi (literally "dance of the East") — is one of the oldest continuously practiced dance traditions in the world. Its origins trace to the fertility rituals and social celebrations of ancient Middle Eastern and North African cultures, with evidence of similar torso-articulated movement appearing in Egyptian tomb paintings dating back thousands of years. The dance evolved through centuries of cultural exchange across the Ottoman Empire, absorbing influences from Turkish, Persian, and Romani traditions.

The defining characteristic of belly dance is isolation — the ability to move one part of the body independently while the rest remains still. A belly dancer can circle their hips in a smooth figure-eight while their shoulders stay perfectly level, or ripple their abdominal muscles in a wave while their arms float in graceful arcs. This isolation technique requires extraordinary muscular control and body awareness that takes years to develop.

Key movements include the shimmy (a rapid vibration of the hips or shoulders), undulations (a rolling wave through the torso), hip drops and lifts (sharp accents created by alternately releasing and engaging the oblique muscles), and snake arms (a flowing wavelike motion through the arms and hands). The footwork is relatively simple — small steps, pivots, and turns — because the emphasis is on what happens above the waist.
